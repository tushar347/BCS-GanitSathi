# Primary Source Literature Matrix (§3.1 Comparison)

| Reference & Citation | Venue | Core Architectural Contribution | Already Established | Required GonitSathi Comparison |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **IntelliCode** [R1]<br>David & Ghosh | EACL 2026 | Multi-agent tutoring with centralized learner state & single-writer validation | Single-writer centralized validation, assistance-sensitive BKT updates | Test evidence sufficiency metrics and bounded diagnostic probing vs single-writer |
| **ScaffoldLM** [R2]<br>Li et al. | ACL 2026 | Stepwise scaffold planning with assess-act-track-record memory loop | Assessment-driven planning memory loop | Test explicit evidence admission and selective commitment on plan-based tutoring |
| **SLOW** [R3]<br>Wei et al. | AIED 2026 | Explicit diagnostic workspace with counterfactual cognitive validation | Internal counterfactual simulation separating diagnosis from action | Compare one real diagnostic probe with internal counterfactual simulation while controlling turns, token/compute budget, latency, and access to the additional student observation |
| **Auditable Release Control** [R4]<br>Kadir | Preprint Aug 2026 | Authorization-aware release mediation with inspectable checks, action-specific fallback, and replayable traces | Reduces pedagogical leakage under declared contracts, but retains residual failures and a safety–utility trade-off | Adopt as a supporting guardrail; report residual leakage and helpfulness rather than claiming prevention |
| **Stepwise Verification** [R5] | EMNLP 2024 | First-error annotation and verification-grounded remediation | First-error localization on reasoning traces | Hold first-error signal constant when evaluating state governance |
| **StratL** [R6] | ACL Findings 2025 | Prompt optimization over state-transition pedagogical graphs | Graph-based pedagogical transition planning | Compare fixed vs adaptive pedagogical controller with identical models |
| **Pedagogical RL** [R7] | EMNLP 2025 | Tutor policy fine-tuning via simulated student interaction | Policy specialization via synthetic multi-turn RL | Separate controller architectural gains from weight fine-tuning |
| **MathTutorBench** [R8] | EMNLP 2025 | Multi-turn tutoring benchmark evaluating dialogue quality | Beyond-accuracy pedagogical evaluation metrics | Adopt rubric dimensions for human audit and simulated dialogue evaluation |
| **BanglaMATH** [R9] | MathNLP 2025 | Grades 6–8 math reasoning benchmark in Bengali | Evaluation of elementary Bengali mathematical reasoning across arithmetic, algebra, geometry, and logic | Use as an external Bengali benchmark reference; evaluate transfer to authentic multi-domain BCS problems without treating it as GonitSathi's training curriculum |
| **BenNumEval** [R10] | Findings of ACL 2025 | Six-task benchmark for numerical reasoning in Bengali | Cross-lingual prompting can improve results, but evaluated LLMs remain below human performance, especially under Bengali-native prompting | Use as an external numerical-reasoning benchmark; validate Bengali numeral normalization and token sensitivity separately |
| **GanitLLM** [R11] | ACL Findings 2026 | Bengali math reasoning via Curriculum-GRPO | Bengali mathematical model fine-tuning with RL | Potential backbone comparator; distinguishes controller from model tuning |
| **DAGGER** [R12]<br>Al Nazi et al. | Preprint 2026 | DISTRACTMATH-BN robustness benchmark and executable computational-graph generation with explicit distractor nodes | Structured intermediate representations improve robustness and efficiency under irrelevant context | Adapt its distractor perturbations for robustness testing; do not treat its computational graphs as evidence for student-diagnostic probe generation |
| **GSM-Plus-BN** [R13] | Preprint July 2026 | Perturbation benchmark for Bengali math reasoning | Linguistic sensitivity of Bengali LLMs to numerical shifts | Informs language and numerical perturbation stress test E5 |
| **Small Language Models are the Future of Agentic AI** [R14]<br>Belcak et al. | arXiv position paper 2025 | Argues that SLMs are economical and suitable for narrow, repetitive agent calls and proposes heterogeneous SLM/LLM systems | Position argument for SLM use in agents, not empirical validation of a fixed parameter range or edge deployment | Use as theoretical motivation for the OE-Agent architecture; establish 1.7B/4B/8B feasibility empirically in E3 |
| **RouteLLM: Learning to Route LLMs with Preference Data** [R15]<br>Ong et al. | ICLR 2025 | Preference-trained query routing between stronger and weaker LLMs | Cost–quality trade-offs from selecting one model per query | Treat as related efficiency literature; E3 remains a matched model-scale study unless a routing arm is added |
| **PAL: Program-Aided Language Models** [R16]<br>Gao et al. | ICML 2023 | Generates Python programs from language problems and delegates calculation to an interpreter | External execution improves reasoning beyond code-style prompting alone; meaningful variables help semantic grounding | Use PAL as an executable-reasoning baseline; compare it with GonitSathi’s restricted verification and evidence-governed diagnosis on invalid, unsupported, and alternative student steps |
| **Knowledge Tracing: Modeling the Acquisition of Procedural Knowledge** [R17]<br>Corbett & Anderson | UMUAI 1995 | Tracks a learned/unlearned state for each production rule from student performance using Bayesian inference | Four parameters—initial mastery, learning, guess, and slip—support performance prediction and mastery-based practice | Use BKT as a longitudinal mastery baseline; compare it with GonitSathi’s episode-level diagnostic causes and test whether provenance-aware evidence, revocable claims, and bounded probes improve safe diagnosis and remediation |
| **MathDial: A Dialogue Tutoring Dataset with Rich Pedagogical Properties Grounded in Math Reasoning Problems** [R18]<br>Macina et al. | Findings of EMNLP 2023 | Collects 3K semi-synthetic math tutoring dialogues by pairing human teachers with LLM-simulated students and annotating scaffolded teacher moves | Fine-tuning improves tutoring quality; smaller models match ChatGPT’s solving success while revealing solutions less often | Compare only with a matched backbone and Bengali/domain adaptation; otherwise treat MathDial as an English-domain tutoring reference rather than a direct Bengali BCS baseline |
